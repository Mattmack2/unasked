## Pareto selection

C05 was selected. It adds a fifth primary domain to the recent publication window if it eventually survives, has unusually strong real-world intervention evidence, and contains both a durable mechanism and an active uncertainty.

Its strongest feature is not the headline climate number. The 2020 sulfur rule changed one input sharply enough that researchers can observe multiple linked consequences—ship tracks, cloud droplet properties, radiative forcing and lightning—against an otherwise noisy atmosphere.

---

# 1. The simple version

Ships historically burned high-sulfur fuel. Sulfur dioxide emitted in exhaust forms sulfate aerosol particles. Over relatively clean ocean air, those particles can act as cloud condensation nuclei, producing clouds with more numerous, smaller droplets.

Smaller droplets generally make a cloud reflect more sunlight. In satellite imagery, strong cases appear as bright linear **ship tracks** running through marine cloud decks.

On 1 January 2020, the International Maritime Organization cut the maximum sulfur content of most marine fuel from **3.5% to 0.5%**. Studies estimate international shipping SO2 emissions fell by roughly 70–80%.

The intended effect was cleaner air and fewer health harms.

The climate side effect was less sulfate aerosol, fewer cloud droplets in affected conditions, darker or less perturbed clouds, and therefore less sunlight reflected back to space. That produces a **positive radiative forcing**: it reveals some greenhouse warming that had previously been masked by pollution.

This does not mean sulfur pollution caused a good climate state. It means greenhouse gases and aerosol pollution were pushing Earth's energy balance in opposite directions, and removing one cooling pollutant without simultaneously removing enough greenhouse gases exposes part of the warming already committed by the latter.

---

# 2. Why this is interesting

The first framing looked like a policy irony:

> cleaner air made warming worse.

That is true in a narrow physical sense and misleading in almost every broader sense.

Sulfur pollution causes large health harms and acid deposition. The 2020 rule was anticipated to reduce premature mortality substantially. The climate problem is not that the pollution was removed. It is that human activity had built a temporary aerosol cooling mask on top of a persistent greenhouse-gas warming problem.

The deeper reason IMO2020 matters is experimental.

Aerosol-cloud interactions are one of the largest uncertainties in climate forcing. Normally emissions change gradually alongside many other variables, making attribution difficult. Shipping created unusually clean geometry: narrow, intense aerosol sources crossing relatively clean oceans. Then a global regulation sharply reduced the sulfur content of that source.

That gives researchers something close to a planetary before/after perturbation—still noisy and uncontrolled, but much more informative than ordinary observational drift.

# 3. Vocabulary graft

## Ship track

A bright, often linear cloud feature created when ship-emitted aerosol changes droplet number and size in marine clouds. Visible tracks are the clearest manifestation, not the full aerosol-cloud effect.

## Cloud condensation nuclei (CCN)

Aerosol particles on which water vapor condenses to form cloud droplets.

## Twomey effect

For a fixed amount of cloud water, increasing cloud droplet number tends to make droplets smaller and the cloud brighter, increasing reflected sunlight.

## Cloud adjustments

Aerosol changes can also alter cloud lifetime, precipitation, liquid-water amount and cloud fraction. These responses are more complex than the first-order Twomey effect.

## Aerosol effective radiative forcing (ERF)

The change in Earth's energy balance caused by an aerosol perturbation after rapid atmospheric adjustments. Positive ERF warms relative to the comparison state; negative ERF cools.

## Aerosol masking / unmasking

Anthropogenic aerosols have offset part of greenhouse warming. Cleaning them up reduces that cooling offset and can temporarily accelerate observed warming even though the cleanup is beneficial for health.

## Marine cloud brightening (MCB)

A proposed climate-intervention approach that deliberately adds particles, usually sea-salt aerosol, to susceptible marine clouds to increase reflectivity. Shipping is often used as an imperfect natural analogue for studying aerosol-cloud sensitivity.

# 4. What physically changed after 2020

The IMO sulfur cap reduced the global maximum from 3.5% sulfur by mass to 0.5% outside designated emission-control areas.

A 2025 Bay of Bengal study cites an estimated decline in international shipping SO2 emissions from 10.4 Tg in 2019 to 3.0 Tg in 2020, about 71%.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41612-025-01256-w

A global machine-learning survey of more than one million ship tracks found a sharp post-2020 decline. Despite an emissions drop around 80%, the number of detectable tracks fell only around 25% in one analysis—already a clue that cloud response is nonlinear and that visible tracks are only a subset of ship influence.

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9565328/

A 2023 study focusing on a southeastern Atlantic shipping corridor detected larger cloud droplets and evidence of reduced cloud brightening after the rule, consistent with fewer aerosol particles reaching the clouds.

Source: https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/23/8259/2023/

---

# 5. A result I did not expect: the lightning changed too

The most surprising branch was not low marine cloud brightness.

Shipping lanes had previously been associated with enhanced lightning over otherwise clean tropical ocean regions. If sulfur-rich ship aerosol really changes deep convective clouds, the 2020 sulfur reduction offered a direct test.

A 2025 peer-reviewed study over the Bay of Bengal found a substantial post-2020 decline in lightning over the shipping lane and linked it to cloud responses to the sulfur regulation. The associated work reports an enhancement falling by more than 40% across a range of atmospheric conditions, alongside a decline in cloud-base droplet number.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41612-025-01256-w

This matters because it extends the natural experiment beyond the easily photographed white line of a ship track. Aerosol perturbations can propagate into deep convective cloud behavior, not just low-cloud albedo.

---

# 6. The visible lines are not the whole forcing

An especially useful 2025 result challenged another intuitive shortcut.

It would be natural to estimate the shipping cloud effect from visible ship tracks. But a Communications Earth & Environment study concluded that **detectable ship tracks account for only about 5% of the aerosol indirect forcing from ship emissions** in its analysis.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02825-w

That means the photogenic features are a biased sample of a much broader diffuse perturbation. A ship plume can alter cloud microphysics without producing a crisp line visible from space.

This changes how to interpret the post-2020 decline. “Fewer ship tracks” is evidence of the perturbation; it is not a complete measurement of the radiative effect.

---

# 7. How much warming did the cleanup expose?

This is where the literature becomes genuinely uncertain.

Several 2024–25 estimates cluster in the same order of magnitude but not at one agreed value.

A four-model plus chemical-transport-model study estimated global effective radiative forcing of about **+0.06 to +0.09 W/m²** from the shipping sulfur reduction.

Source: https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/24/13361/2024/

A machine-learning analysis of low-cloud regions estimated **+0.074 ± 0.005 W/m²** associated with changes in shortwave cloud radiative effect, while emphasizing that natural cloud variability makes the signal hard to detect.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-024-01911-9

Other climate-model studies estimate around **+0.12 to +0.14 W/m²** and global mean warming of roughly a few hundredths of a degree, with larger regional effects.

Sources:
- https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2024GL109077
- https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/24/13681/2024/
- https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2024EF005011

These are not trivial forcings. One multi-model paper notes that +0.06 to +0.09 W/m² is comparable to the forcing from roughly two to three years of recent CO2 concentration growth.

But this does **not** establish that IMO2020 caused the exceptional global warmth of 2023.

Some papers argue it made a substantial contribution to the anomaly; others emphasize that the signal is hard to separate from El Niño, ocean variability, greenhouse trends and other forcing changes. Even papers estimating a meaningful shipping contribution state that it cannot account for all observed warming.

That uncertainty is a central Turn 2 target, not a footnote.

# 8. Genealogy: ships were an accidental aerosol-cloud experiment before 2020

Ship tracks have been visible in weather-satellite imagery for more than half a century.

They became useful because they create a natural contrast experiment: a polluted plume and nearby relatively unperturbed marine cloud can experience similar large-scale meteorology.

Over time the field used ship tracks to study the **Twomey effect**, cloud adjustments and aerosol indirect forcing. Machine learning later made it possible to catalog more than one million tracks globally rather than relying on small manually identified samples.

The strange historical inversion is that an air-pollution source became one of atmospheric science's clearest laboratories for understanding aerosol-cloud interactions.

Then IMO2020 changed the laboratory input almost overnight.

The regulation was therefore not the beginning of the science. It was a rare perturbation imposed on a decades-old natural experiment.

---

# 9. Public health is not the counterargument; it is part of the system

A 2018 Nature Communications analysis published before IMO2020 estimated that lower-sulfur marine fuel would substantially reduce ship-related premature mortality and childhood asthma while reducing aerosol cooling.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-02774-9

The correct mental model is therefore not a binary choice between clean air and climate.

Sulfur aerosol is short-lived. CO2 is long-lived. If sulfur is removed while CO2 remains high, the masking disappears quickly. A durable solution requires reducing the pollutant that harms health **and** the greenhouse gases whose warming it had partly hidden.

Calling the sulfur cap a climate mistake would confuse a temporary symptom mask with the underlying forcing.

---

# 10. Why this is relevant to marine cloud brightening

Marine cloud brightening proposes intentionally adding particles to susceptible marine clouds to increase their reflectivity.

Shipping emissions are not a clean prototype: sulfate chemistry, particle size distributions, plume structure and co-emitted pollutants differ from proposed sea-salt spraying. But the shipping perturbation provides real observations of how marine clouds respond to added—and now removed—aerosol.

Two lessons appear especially durable:

1. **cloud response is strongly state-dependent and noisy.** The 2024 detection study found the global forcing relatively large but observationally hard to distinguish from natural variability.
2. **visible tracks are a poor proxy for total forcing.** Much of the aerosol-cloud effect can occur without obvious linear cloud features.

A separate 2024 Nature Climate Change modeling study also found that regional MCB interventions effective under present climate conditions could become less effective or produce remote side effects in a warmer future state.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-024-02046-7

So IMO2020 does not prove MCB will work safely. It makes the measurement and attribution problem harder to dismiss.

# 11. What is established vs inferred

## FOUNDATION / ESTABLISHED

- Sulfur-rich ship emissions create sulfate aerosol and can brighten marine clouds.
- The IMO2020 sulfur cap sharply reduced shipping sulfur emissions.
- Detectable ship tracks fell after implementation.
- Large-scale cloud microphysical changes have been observed in at least some major shipping corridors.
- Removing aerosol cooling produces positive radiative forcing relative to the polluted baseline.

## CURRENT / STRONG

- Multiple models and observational approaches estimate a positive global forcing from IMO2020 of order ~0.05–0.15 W/m².
- A 2025 study links reduced sulfur emissions to a large decline in shipping-lane lightning over the Bay of Bengal.
- Diffuse aerosol-cloud effects can dominate over visually detectable ship tracks.

## FRONTIER / INFERRED

- The exact fraction of 2023–26 exceptional warmth attributable to IMO2020.
- How much current aerosol cleanup will accelerate near-term warming globally as air quality improves elsewhere.
- How faithfully shipping constrains deliberate marine cloud brightening efficacy and side effects.

# 12. Strongest reasons this candidate could fail in Turn 2

1. The warming-attribution literature may be too model-dependent to support a strong reader-facing claim about recent temperatures.
2. COVID-era shipping changes could confound some before/after observations, though major studies explicitly try to account for this.
3. Regional cloud responses may not scale cleanly to global forcing.
4. “Inadvertent geoengineering termination shock” may be rhetorically stronger than the actual perturbation warrants.
5. Public communication can easily mutate the result into “pollution was protecting us,” erasing the health cost and the difference between short-lived aerosol cooling and long-lived greenhouse forcing.
6. Shipping aerosol is not compositionally or operationally equivalent to proposed sea-salt MCB.

# 13. Cheap falsification / reversal conditions

The central article survives if independent methods continue to agree on three points: post-2020 sulfur reduction, observable cloud/aerosol response, and positive radiative forcing.

Retire or radically narrow if Turn 2 finds that the apparent cloud changes are mostly explained by shipping-volume/COVID changes, that global forcing estimates collapse under newer emissions data, or that the lightning result does not survive peer review or alternate meteorological controls.

The 2023-warming attribution should be removed entirely if it proves too unstable; the deeper natural-experiment story does not require it.

# 14. What changed during the research

1. **The policy-irony framing got weaker.** “Cleaner air caused warming” is technically provocative but conceptually shallow.
2. **The natural-experiment framing got stronger.** IMO2020 is useful because it sharply perturbed a historically important aerosol-cloud laboratory.
3. **Visible ship tracks stopped looking like the main object.** A 2025 paper estimates they represent only a small fraction of total ship aerosol indirect forcing.
4. **Lightning opened a second mechanism window.** The sulfur reduction appears to have changed deep convective behavior, not just low-cloud brightness.
5. **The climate number became less central.** Estimates cluster in the same order of magnitude, but attribution of specific recent temperature anomalies remains contested.
6. **The health tradeoff became clearer.** The right inference is not to preserve sulfur pollution; it is to expect warming to become more visible as short-lived cooling pollution is removed unless greenhouse forcing falls too.
7. **MCB became a measurement story rather than an endorsement.** The shipping experiment shows both that aerosol can alter clouds and that detecting/predicting the resulting climate response is difficult.
