## Adversarial check

The central conceptual claim survives: Swanson explicitly framed published but disconnected bodies of knowledge as capable of supporting new, previously unstated inferences. Later historical reviews identify this as the foundational “undiscovered public knowledge” / literature-based discovery program.

The strongest correction is semantic. Saying “the internet contains a fact nobody wrote down” is a compelling hook, but scientifically it is too strong if read literally. What exists in the documents are premises and relationships; the new endpoint is an **inference or hypothesis**. The published article should make that distinction quickly instead of laundering a metaphor into an epistemic claim.

The second caveat is evaluation. Modern reviews have criticized LBD evaluation practices, including heavy reuse of canonical discoveries. This does not erase the field, but it argues against presenting LBD as a solved automated-discovery engine.

## Packaging decision

Keep the hook, immediately qualify “fact” as shorthand for a connection/inference, and make the real story the existence of a decades-old field for finding knowledge between literatures.
